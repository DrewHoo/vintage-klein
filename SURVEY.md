## Brand notes

### Milwaukee
Period merch says **"Milwaukee Electric Tools"** — modern says "Milwaukee Tool." Best era signals: **K-Products** and **Swingster** tags, made in USA. Dealer co-brands (Schlecter, Mallory, City Supply) are common and legit. Only ~5 real 90s tees and ~3 crews live right now; hats and K-Products satin bombers ($39–115) are where the vintage supply actually is. Watch for sellers leaning on "Nothing But Heavy Duty" over Gildan blanks.

### Klein Tools
Vintage shirts barely exist: a $45 NOS 70s Russell Athletic orange ad tee (size S, [eBay 800629489184](https://www.ebay.com/itm/800629489184)) is the best shirt on the market, period. One possible: orange "Vintage Klein Tools Shirt" on [Mercari $40](https://www.mercari.com/us/item/m68343431149/), size listed "One Size" — worth a pit-to-pit ask, but the seller's Depop shop has a bad condition-accuracy review. Zero vintage sweatshirts anywhere. The real vintage Klein market is **trucker hats**: 1982 125th and 1987 130th anniversary caps $10–35, orange rope/foam truckers $20–50. This is a saved-search brand, not a sweep brand.

### DeWalt
Racing timeline correction (sellers get this wrong constantly): **Sadler/Dotter mid-90s Busch → Kenseth #17 Chevy 1998–99 → Kenseth #17 Roush Ford 2000–09 → Kenseth #20 Gibbs 2013+ (not vintage)**. Kurt Busch was never DeWalt. Many "90s" listings are 2001–05 Kenseth — date by driver, car, tag, and stitch, not title. Pre-Kenseth single-stitch Sadler tees are the sleeper buy ($19–24 on Depop vs $45–89 on eBay). Cross-listing arbitrage is real: same items 30–60% cheaper on eBay/Mercari than Grailed.

### Snap-on & Mac Tools
The backbone of the niche. Snap-on: eagle/flag graphics, hot-rod "Tool For Every Toy," NHRA, anniversary tees; real 80s/90s single-stitch at $25–60 with plenty of L/XL. Mac Tools: racing-dominated — NHRA Gatornationals tees (Jerzees, 1995–2005) reliably $18–30; Davey Allison crews $40–80; Ernie Irvan is the grail name ($278+ at specialist shops). Watch for Y2K Gildan dealer promos sold as "vintage NOS" ("Steel Series," "Too Fast for Rust").

## Authentication cheat sheet

- **Single stitch** = pre-~1996 (late-90s transition pieces: single cuffs + double hem). Necessary but not sufficient — some modern blanks are single-stitch.
- **Tubular body, no side seams** = vintage signal. Side seams on a claimed pre-95 shirt = reprint.
- **Tag material:** thick cotton 70s → thin/papery early-mid 80s → shiny nylon mid-80s–mid-90s → multilingual double tags late-90s+ → long printed tags 2010s+.
- **Blank date ranges:** Screen Stars 1980–90 (Screen Stars Best ~1986→90s); Hanes 50/50 1982–93, Fifty-Fifty 1985–98; Jerzees grid tag late-80s–mid-90s (thin boxed tag ~1996–97, then Mexico); Tultex USA through early 2000s.
- **Made in USA** mostly means mid-80s–mid-90s (NAFTA pushed production to Central America from 1994). Honduras/Nicaragua/Bangladesh = late 90s at the earliest.
- **Print feel:** old plastisol sits on top and cracks along the knit; DTG reprints feel flat and embedded. Puff print ≈ 80s/90s but is back in modern production.
- **Fit:** 90s tees run a size big and boxy. **Hunt by pit-to-pit** — for a modern L/XL wearer, target ~22–24".
- **Reprint traps:** (1) print-on-demand on Gildan/Bella+Canvas/Next Level with "S + more" size menus, "Located in Indonesia," $7–22; (2) the brands' own retro lines — Klein 150th/Est. 1857 reissues, Snap-on "Faded USA Made"/"Retro Racing," Milwaukee "1924" heritage; (3) new print on genuine old blank — nearly undetectable, so distrust listings hiding the tag; (4) bootlegs rarely carry ®/© marks.
- **Always check eBay item specifics against the title** — multiple live listings have size or "Vintage: No" contradictions in their own specifics.

## Standing-hunt setup

**Channels:** eBay saved searches (Clothing category 11450, email alerts) are the main engine; Depop for cheap single-stitch finds; Mercari for cross-listing discounts; Grailed is a markup market (check eBay first for the same item); **Gem (gem.app)** aggregates all of them plus indie shops. Specialist shops worth watching: Vintage Rare USA (racing), Lost Boys Vintage, Joint Custody DC, Vintage Snapback Warehouse (hats), The Vintage Twin.
